## MinoDriven Primary Sources

### M1. Effective Learning of Good Code

- URL: https://speakerdeck.com/minodriven/effective-learning-of-good-code
- Verified: 2026-07-16; relevant slides 18, 20, 26, 31-42, 45, and 48-50.
- Adopted: practitioners work on familiar or representative code, identify and verbalize a problem, redesign, explain why the design addresses it, and receive questions or feedback. Production code is preferred in the example, while generated material is allowed when none is available.
- Limit: the five topics, eight steps, duration, participant count, individual mode, from-scratch implementation, and reported outcomes describe one DMM practice. They are not universal workshop requirements or independently measured effectiveness. Slide 23's learning-pyramid rationale is not adopted.

### M2. Ghosts of Technical Debt

- URL: https://speakerdeck.com/minodriven/ghosts-of-technical-debt
- Verified: 2026-07-16; relevant slides 16 and 34-36.
- Adopted: hands-on work with realistic code can support design learning; recognizing debt depends on understanding a more changeable target structure.
- Limit: practitioner guidance, not a validated workshop specification or scoring method.

## Research Sources

### A1. Freeman et al., Active Learning in STEM

- URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24821756/
- Relevant pages: 8410-8413.
- Adopted: across 225 undergraduate STEM studies, varied active-learning conditions had better average assessment and failure outcomes than traditional lecture.
- Limit: the meta-analysis does not identify one best activity, intensity, duration, or professional software workshop format.

### A2. Chi and Wylie, ICAP Framework

- URL: https://education.asu.edu/sites/default/files/lcl/chiwylie2014icap_2.pdf
- Relevant pages: 219-220, 232-236, and 238-240.
- Adopted: generating explanations is constructive activity; substantive co-construction can be interactive; merely grouping people does not make an activity interactive.
- Limit: observable behavior is only a proxy for engagement, and task, prior knowledge, assessment, and domain can change outcomes.

### A3. Hattie and Timperley, The Power of Feedback

- URL: https://doi.org/10.3102/003465430298487
- Relevant pages: 81-96.
- Adopted: feedback effects vary; feedback tied to goals, tasks, and process is more defensible than person-focused praise or judgment.
- Limit: classroom review evidence does not require a fixed reviewer, facilitator, score, or peer-feedback ceremony.

### A4. Baldwin and Ford, Transfer of Training

- URL: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1744-6570.1988.tb00632.x
- Relevant pages: 63-105.
- Adopted: transfer concerns generalization to work and maintenance over time; trainee, design, and work environment all matter.
- Limit: this general review supports only separating session performance from workplace transfer or adoption.

### A5. Masters, Edgar Dale's Pyramid of Learning

- URL: https://doi.org/10.3109/0142159X.2013.800636
- Relevant pages: e1584 and e1591-e1593.
- Adopted: familiar retention percentages and their attribution are unsupported and should not be used.
- Limit: this rejects fixed percentages, not active learning itself.
